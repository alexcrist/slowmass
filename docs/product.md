# Product contract

Status: core MVP and account deletion are implemented. See the implementation plan for remaining work and verification gaps.

## Experience

A mobile-first place to share single photos with friends. The feed is reverse chronological, finite per page, and contains the viewer’s own posts and posts from authors they currently follow through an accepted request. There is no recommendation algorithm or engagement ranking.

- Sign in through a one-time email link that confirms email ownership. No passwords. Google sign-in is implemented alongside email links; the owner approved the email-first layout and the controls/backend are deployed; completed real-provider sign-in verification remains pending. Both methods must reach the same account and preserve invitation provenance. Event joining asks only for a name and generates an internal username. Entering the photo app explains that account setup requires a username and profile photo, then asks members for both on one screen with Finish; later changes live in Edit profile. Until they add a profile photo, members can use events, Activity, Settings, Find, and other members’ profile previews (see Events); the photo unlocks posting, following, and the feed. Members without a photo are hidden from member profiles, discovery, and connection lists. Choosing a file opens a crop editor with touch dragging and pinch-to-zoom, plus keyboard and slider controls; confirming the crop prepares a draft during account setup, and Finish saves it. In Edit profile, confirming the crop immediately saves the replacement. Later updates replace the photo rather than removing it.
- Every member after the configured first member joins by signing in through an invite link or an event’s URL from an active member with a profile photo. Texting a link is how members invite someone. Each member has one invite link, shown with a Copy button from the mail icon beside Settings on their own profile or the Invite to slowmass row in Settings. Opening the owner’s dialog refreshes the current link to seven days from now without changing its URL or version. After expiry or revocation, opening the dialog issues a new version and the old one stops admitting people. Recipient visits never extend it. Preserve who invited whom and when permanently, including minimal tombstones through deletion, so a burst of sign-ups can be traced to the link that admitted it and shut down: an invite-link join records the link’s owner as inviter and the exact link version; an event-link join records the event creator as inviter, the event, and the exact link version. Joining rechecks the exact link version and its owner’s eligibility (and, for an event, that it is published, not cancelled, and not over); failures never fall back to another inviter. Existing members sign in through either kind of link without changing attribution. Email addresses remain private. Email invitations were removed on October 17, 2026; their records remain for provenance and outstanding ones were revoked, along with that day’s invite links (migration 0030). Invite links returned on October 18, 2026 (migration 0032).
- Usernames can be changed in Edit profile. Changing one releases the old name, changes the profile link, and preserves content and relationships by account ID. No old-name redirects are retained.
- Each account has an avatar and plain-text bio. Treat profile photos as public identity information; setup and editing do not promise visibility limited to signed-in members. Signed-in, unblocked members can view a profile's username, name, avatar, and their follow-request state. Bios, posts, and accepted Following/Followers lists require ownership or a current accepted follow. Pending requests and blocked-account lists remain owner-only. Connection lists hide inactive, unfinished, and viewer-blocked accounts. Anonymous profile URLs reveal no profile data. Profiles have an identity header and a paginated photo grid. Your own profile and profiles you follow include Posts, Following, and Followers tabs. Your own profile also has the invite-link mail icon and a Settings gear at the top right; account administration and installation help live in Settings, also accessible from the desktop sidebar.
- Members who joined through a former personal email invitation follow their inviter automatically once both have profiles; subsequent unfollowing or blocking is respected, and edits and sign-ins never restore a relationship. Invite and event links never create automatic follows; private posts still require normal accepted follow requests.
- Find people by name or username with case-insensitive fuzzy matching (letters in order, allowing skipped letters), a three-character minimum, and a 350 ms debounce. An empty search shows a shuffled member directory with explicit pages and stable ordering during pagination. Blocked and inactive members are excluded. Searches are limited to 30 per member per minute.
- Send, cancel, accept, or deny follow requests. Unfollow, remove a follower, and block an account, each requiring confirmation before changing the relationship. On another member’s profile, Unfollow and Block account live in the overflow menu. Blocking removes relationships and pending requests in both directions and prevents new interactions. Unblocking does not restore follows.
- Publish exactly one still photo with an optional plain-text caption. The composer currently omits the photo-description field; existing stored descriptions remain accessible to screen readers. Crop before publishing with a freeform rectangle, movable edges/corners, and keyboard controls; preserve the chosen aspect ratio. Show a clear unsupported-format message when conversion cannot be performed.
- Show own published posts in the home feed alongside accepted follows, using the same chronological order and pagination. Own posts also remain on the profile, which opens after posting. Do not create a synthetic self-follow.
- Feed cards show the author's username and avatar, linking to their profile. Like/unlike and comment controls use accessible icons and positive counts. Clicking the like count opens a paginated modal of members who liked the post. Comments are flat and paginated, with avatars, usernames, compact relative times, and their own likes and likers modal; there are no replies. Authors can delete their comments; post owners can delete comments on their posts. The trash icon opens a confirmation before deletion. Owners can edit captions or delete posts through a top-right three-dot menu in feed and focused views. Images and publication times cannot be edited.
- Opening a photo from the feed, a profile grid, or Activity navigates to its own post page with comments expanded. On desktop screens where a focused photo does not fill the screen width, tapping or clicking the photo opens a larger image-only viewer; full-width mobile photos have no enlargement action. A contextual back link returns to the originating list page; direct links fall back to Feed. Comment fields use a pill-shaped composer with a circular send button and automatically growing multiline input.
- Show in-app notifications for invitees joining, follow requests, new followers, likes, comments, and event activity, with safe links. Acceptance replaces a request with new-follower activity; invitation follows notify both people once both have profile photos. Existing accepted follows are not replayed. Notify the inviter (the invite link’s owner, or the event creator for event-link joins) once the new member adds a profile photo. The whole joined row includes their private avatar and links to their current profile. No historical join events are backfilled; current activity visibility respects blocking, deletion and incomplete profiles. New-follower rows open the current profile and disappear if that relationship is removed. A numbered Activity badge counts unread items across all types. An item becomes read after its summary is at least half visible for 250 continuous milliseconds in the active tab; opening Activity alone does not clear unseen items. Read state is independent of follow-request decisions, so seen pending requests do not keep the badge lit. There is no manual mark-as-read action. Group consecutive likes on a post into one item; other activity in between starts a new item, which reads “N more people like your photo.” Display authoritative current counts. Like groups from before October 17, 2026 stay as they were. Activity is one newest-first, paginated timeline, with requests accepted or denied in place. Like/comment rows include a post thumbnail and open the post when selected. Web Push is off by default and can be enabled or disabled per device in Settings → Notifications or from the device-setup suggestion; that page shows whether push works on this device and walks through turning it on, including Home Screen installation on iPhone/iPad. There, members also choose push and/or email for each kind of activity: follow requests, new followers, people they invited joining, likes, comments, RSVPs to events they host, event messages, update blasts, cancelled events, co-host invitations, and people accepting their co-host invitations. Defaults keep earlier behavior: push for everything except likes, email only for update blasts and co-host invitations. Alerts and emails describe the activity (“alice commented on your photo.”, with the comment) and open its target; push uses the matching Activity item. Several likes on a photo arrive as one alert after 15 minutes; any one notification alerts at most once an hour, so re-sent requests and changed RSVPs can't spam anyone. Email skips activity already read in the app, except update blasts. Each email's unsubscribe link turns off email for that category only. Accepting a follow request doesn't alert or badge the person accepting. Receiving an alert does not mark it read. Signing out stops new pushes. No SMS or email digests.
- Use explicit Newer/Older page navigation with the page number, repeated above posts after the first page; preserve navigation state, and show a short horizontal line with an accessible label at the end of posts. Load images near the viewport without automatically fetching another page.
- Provide logout and account deletion. Deletion immediately removes visibility and schedules retryable object cleanup. Document backup retention separately from live deletion.

## Privacy semantics

Members retain ownership of their content. Slowmass uses their data only to provide the features they choose and to operate/protect the app. Never sell or rent it, share it with advertisers/data brokers, send it to AI tools, use it for model training, or authorize others to do so. Operational AI assistance must use synthetic data or content-free aggregates. The public privacy notice lives in `public/privacy/index.html`; update it before introducing new data uses or providers.

Access is evaluated at request time. A removed follower cannot fetch more posts, bios, connection lists, or post media, including through an old image URL. Content already downloaded cannot be recalled. Blocking also hides the blocked account's profile, avatar, and interactions, and notification rendering rechecks current access. Avatars require active membership and no block in either direction; an accepted follow is not required.

In-app actions need friendly errors and retry behavior. Losing a connection during publication must not create duplicate posts or lose the ability to determine whether publication succeeded.

## Deferred

Native app-store releases, public profiles, contact import, videos, animated images, albums, stories, reels, DMs, hashtags, location features, filters, resharing, recommendations, ads, payments, email digests, and offline private-photo storage.

## Product quality

Use a restrained photo-first design with a single-column feed, clear navigation, and accessible controls. Verify narrow phones through desktop layouts, keyboard and touch flows, reduced motion, screen-reader labels, and slow-network states. Never depend on color alone to communicate state.

Installation guidance and in-app install prompts are limited to iPhone, iPad, and Android. Desktop uses the website without an installation flow or device-setup suggestion.

Four-hour magic links are the currently deployed sign-in method. A sign-in lasts 60 days after the app was last opened on that device. After requesting a link, all login paths lead with “Open the sign-in link in your email.” followed by the address, spam-folder reminder, and expiry/single-use details. Returning to the app or reconnecting rechecks the current session without polling; only failed checks offer a retry. Installed apps can have separate browser sessions. Every email confirmation offers Send a new link to the same address. Failed or expired email links show an email form with a visible Send a new link button; the address is requested again rather than stored in the URL. Newly issued emails retain the original invite/event link for recovery across browsers, and resending rechecks that invitation. An expired or revoked invitation still requires a current link from its owner. Only installed apps show a collapsed “Trouble signing in?” helper for pasting an unused link. No account-switch, email-code, or password flow is introduced.

An email without an account stays on the welcome form with a message to ask a member for an invite link. Only an active member, a person signing in through a working invite or event link, or the configured first member can advance to the email-link instructions. Signed-out visitors to an invite link see only that they’re invited to slowmass and an email field; visitors to an event URL see its cover photo, title, and date, followed by an email form. The event description, exact time, location, and host information require sign-in; invite links disclose no inviter information. A link that no longer works says so and falls back to normal sign-in for members.

New-follower Activity offers Follow back only when no outgoing request or accepted follow exists. It sends a normal follow request, requiring acceptance before private posts become visible. Members can dismiss the prompt across devices without removing or marking the notification read. Dismissal belongs to that follower event; a later new follow can offer a fresh prompt. Follow requests and new-follower items both show the member’s private avatar and inline right-side actions.

Onboarding first asks for a name. People who joined through an event stay on it afterward; everyone else goes on to choose a username and profile photo on one screen with Finish. Members can leave setup for Events and finish later. Every name and profile-setup screen also offers Sign out, so completing setup is not required to leave the session. Finishing account setup opens the full app directly; installation and notifications are optional choices in Settings. Android can enable either independently; iPhone/iPad must open a Home Screen app before enabling push; desktop omits installation. Adding a photo never requests notification permission.

Right after adding a profile photo, phones and tablets show one device-setup suggestion at the top of Activity, styled like an activity item and counted once in the badge until seen on that device. iPhone/iPad browsers are asked to add slowmass to the Home Screen as a first step, with numbered steps in a dialog; adding it does not enable notifications by itself. The Home Screen app then asks to turn notifications on, which shows the system permission prompt. Android asks to turn notifications on, then to install the app while the browser offers it. Each step has a × dismiss control, like Follow back. Dismissing is permanent for the account on every device; blocking the permission prompt or dismissing the install prompt opened from the suggestion also counts as No. Existing members who joined before October 14, 2026 get the suggestion once; members who already finished or dismissed the former onboarding setup screen are not asked again.

## Events

Status: implemented. The client and server event READMEs describe current behavior; the implementation plan tracks remaining verification.

Partiful-style events help friends meet up without changing the photo-only feed.

- **Contents.** One cover photo, a title, a description with basic inline formatting (bold, italic, strikethrough), plain-text location (no maps, geotagging or nearby discovery), start time in the event's time zone with an optional end, hosts, a guest list, and an activity log. Hosts and guests answer Going, Maybe, or Can't go, shown with 🪩, 🎲, and 🫠. A saved answer collapses the other options; tapping the chosen answer reveals them again without unselecting it or sending another RSVP. Changing to a different answer collapses the row after a successful save. Only members with a profile photo create events. Creating and editing happen on a preview of the event page: selecting the cover, title, date and time, location, or description edits it in place, and Cancel or Save at the bottom leaves the page. Hosts can edit details, replace the cover, add and remove hosts, and cancel. Detail edits are silent. Hosts can send update blasts: a message that reaches everyone who answered and the other hosts in Activity and, by default, by email (the host gets a copy). Cancellation still notifies everyone who answered.
- **Cancel or delete.** Cancelling notifies participants and keeps the event readable. Only the creator can delete an event, including a cancelled or past one, through the overflow menu and confirmation. Deletion removes it from everyone’s Events and Activity, disables its link and media access, and sends no notification. There is no restore action. Invitation provenance and already admitted accounts remain intact.
- **One URL.** Each event has one shareable URL, `/event/<link>`, with an unguessable credential. Existing `/#event/<link>` URLs still open the same event; newly copied links support messaging-app previews containing only its title and processed cover. Events are unlisted: no event directory, search, feed, profile listing, or follower access reveals them. A member must create an event or open its link before it can appear in their account; a member co-host invitation also shares guest access and places the event in the recipient’s Events list. Preview responses request no search indexing. The credential and invitation version do not change when upgrading the URL format. Only hosts (the creator and accepted co-hosts) see the mail icon beside the title and Invite people in the overflow menu, which open it in a dialog with a Copy button and “Send the link to your friends to invite them.”; it is also what the address bar shows on the event page, for hosts and guests alike. Signed out, the URL previews its processed cover photo, title, and date in the event’s time zone, then asks for an email to see details and RSVP. Description, exact time, location, and host information require sign-in. Signing in through it, or opening it while signed in, records the link version for that member and shows the details, cover, and hosts. Before answering, a locked Guests section explains “RSVP to see who’s going.” Any answer, including Can’t go, adds the guest list, answer counts, and activity log. Answers close when the event is cancelled or over (at its end time, or twelve hours after it starts), and the link then admits no new people. Cancelling is how a host stops a link that spread too far.

- **Appearance.** On Create and Edit event, matching Color theme and Fonts buttons open dialogs where hosts choose one of nine color themes (five light and four evening) and, independently, one of seven font pairings, including Original. Appearance previews immediately. New-event selections save with creation; existing-event selections save independently for everyone viewing that event. Colors cover the full app shell, text, icons, controls, borders, menus, dialogs, and wordmark while creating, viewing, or editing the event, including the signed-out link preview. Fonts change only the page’s headings; the logo and navigation keep slowmass’s own typefaces. Leaving the event restores normal slowmass styling. Changes are silent. Photos and avatars retain their colors; fonts are locally hosted. The Fonts dialog previews each pairing in its own typefaces; other event screens use only the active pairing.

- **Calendar copies.** Signed-in viewers can use Add to calendar, with a calendar-plus icon distinct from the date icon, beneath the location and above the hosts, who have a crown icon beside them. The dialog contains its title, close control, and Google Calendar, Apple Calendar, and Outlook choices with provider icons beside their labels. Google and Outlook open a prefilled calendar entry, and Apple provides a calendar file to import. These are one-time copies, with a one-hour duration if the host set no end time. They include event details and an access-controlled link back, without the invitation credential or guest/host data. Cancelled events and signed-out previews omit the action.

- **Co-hosts.** Add hosts in Edit event opens host management; changes save immediately without discarding unsaved event details, and cancelling event edits does not undo them. Any host can search accepted followers and following and invite an active member with a profile photo. Search and pending invitations identify people by name and username, never email. Sending shares guest access and shows “Asked to co-host” in the recipient’s Events list. It also notifies the recipient (“Alice asked you to co-host Dinner.”) in Activity, with Accept and a confirmed Decline in the row, and by push and email by default. The recipient must accept before becoming a host; declining preserves guest access. Accepting keeps the row as “You’re co-hosting Dinner with Alice.” and tells the inviting host “Bob is now co-hosting Dinner.” (push only by default). Declining tells no one. The invitation leaves Activity once it’s declined, withdrawn, or no longer usable (event cancelled or over, sender no longer hosting, connection removed), and the hosting record leaves when its member stops hosting. A later invitation to an event the member declined arrives already read, with no push or email. Acceptance rechecks the connection to the inviting host, profile completion, active memberships, blocks, host authority, event state, and link version. An event has at most ten hosts including pending member invitations. The creator always hosts; any host can remove other hosts, and co-hosts can stop hosting. Email co-host invitations are no longer actionable and their addresses are cleaned up. Existing co-hosts keep their roles.
- **New people.** The URL is also an invitation to slowmass. Signing in through it by email creates a membership with the event creator as inviter, recording the event and link version—useful for tracing mass sign-ups to one link. New members then give only a name, on the same preview of the event, before its details appear. Existing members signing in through it keep their attribution.
- **Members without a photo.** They can use their events, Activity, Settings (set up profile, edit name, installation, notifications, sign out, delete account), Find, and other members’ profile previews. Previews show the username, name, avatar, and Request to follow button; selecting it opens account setup. Bios, posts, and connection lists remain unavailable, including for legacy automatic follows. Event screens and event Activity identify people by name, not username. They stay hidden from search, the directory, and connection lists, and cannot post or follow. Adding a profile photo unlocks the full app.
- **Navigation.** The phone bottom bar is Feed, Find, New post, Events, Profile. Activity, with its unread badge, sits at the right of the wordmark header on every phone page; on your own profile it is one row above the Settings icon. The header still scrolls with the page. The desktop sidebar keeps Activity and adds Events. Members without a photo have the same bottom bar and desktop sidebar. Find and other members’ profiles open normally. Feed, New post, and the member’s own unfinished Profile remain tappable without lock badges; each opens profile setup without reading private photo-app data. Request to follow on another profile opens the same setup flow, returning to that profile after completion without automatically sending a request. A prompt explains that using the rest of slowmass requires choosing a username and adding a profile photo, along with private profiles and follow approval. It offers primary Continue and secondary Back to event buttons (Back to events on direct entry). Continue opens one screen with a profile-photo picker, username field, and primary Finish button at the bottom. Cropping selects a draft; Finish saves the username and photo and continues to the selected destination. Choosing a new username updates an own-profile destination too. Mobile Settings remains in the header beside Activity. Event pages, including creating and editing one, have no back link, since Events is always one tap away.
- **Notifications.** Activity, plus push and email by each member’s notification settings, as for other activity. Hosts hear about everyone else's current answer, including co-hosts', and every message; guests hear host messages, update blasts, and cancellation. A host’s Send update blast action adds its message to Activity for every other host and everyone who RSVP’d (including Maybe and Can’t go); by default it is also emailed, and pushed to opted-in devices. Each event can send three update blasts a day. Detail edits produce no activity entries or alerts.
- **Guest privacy.** Hosts and people who answered see each other's names, and avatars when present. Blocking the creator denies the event; other blocks hide each person from the other.
- **Limits.** Ten new events per member per day, 500 guest answers and ten hosts per event, and 100 event messages per member per day.
- **Out of scope.** Plus-ones, capacity limits, waitlists, ticketing or payments, public or discoverable events, email or SMS reminders, and event photo albums.
