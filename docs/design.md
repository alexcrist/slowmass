# Interface guidance

The interface uses a white canvas with neutral text and navigation, keeping colored surfaces away from photos. The October 11, 2026 direction replaces the green header and off-white background while retaining the lowercase script wordmark and Douglas Fir primary buttons. Copy remains functional, without slogans or marketing messages.

The app name is always styled `slowmass` in lowercase, including browser titles, installed-app names, accessible labels, notifications, and email sender names and copy. The existing `sm` favicon design stays unchanged.

## Type and color

- Caveat bold: the text-only `slowmass` wordmark.
- Source Sans 3: headings, controls, captions, and body text. Use 400 and 600 weights with system fallbacks.
- White `#ffffff`: page, header, sidebar, forms, and photo surrounds.
- Ink `#171717`: wordmark, body text, navigation, and interaction icons.
- Gray `#737373`: secondary text; light gray `#e5e5e5`: boundaries.
- Neutral `#f5f5f5`: navigation hover/active state and photo loading surfaces.
- Douglas Fir `#214c3b`: primary action buttons only, with white labels.

Both fonts are served locally from `public/fonts`, with their SIL Open Font Licenses included. No third-party font or image requests. The reusable wordmark is also available at `/slowmass-wordmark.svg` as standalone vector outlines.

## Layout and interaction

On mobile, profile tabs share the identity header's 16px side inset, including their divider and active underline. The photo grid remains edge to edge.

Member pages start with their content rather than a visible page-title label. Keep a visually hidden heading for screen-reader navigation, without an empty heading row. Onboarding retains visible instructional headings.

Content is left aligned inside a centered column up to 640px wide; the desktop feed narrows to 520px. Sign-in uses a smaller form. Feed, Find, New post, Activity, and Profile are the five primary destinations. At 900px and above, a fixed 72px icon sidebar expands to 216px on pointer hover or keyboard focus, revealing labels without shifting the content. The Caveat wordmark holds its initial `s` in place while the compact `m` fades into a softly revealed `lowmass` tail, timed with the sidebar expansion; reduced motion disables both transitions. Settings sits at the bottom of the desktop sidebar. Each link retains an accessible name while its visible label is collapsed. The sidebar scrolls on short screens and its transition respects reduced motion.

Below 900px, the white header shows the wordmark, and the five primary destinations form a fixed bottom bar with labels and safe-area spacing. Profiles show username, name, avatar, permitted bio, and a three-column photo grid. An Invite friends shortcut sits immediately to the left of the Settings gear beside the avatar/name group on your own profile, with both groups top aligned in the same row. Your profile and profiles you follow have Posts (default), Following, and Followers tabs beneath the identity header; tabs keep the header mounted and paginate their content. Other members’ connection rows link to profiles without relationship-management buttons. Pending and non-followers see the private-account state instead of tabs. Use a single divider below the tabs with an active underline; no separate rule above them. The separate Settings page contains editing, invitations, relationship administration, installation, sign-out, and account deletion. Activity is one paginated timeline, newest first, mixing requests, new followers, grouped likes, and comments with Newer/Older pagination. A like group never jumps over other items; a later group on the same post says “N more people like your photo.” Requests have inline Accept/Deny controls; new-follower rows say “started following you” with a profile link and no decision buttons. Omit unread labels and mark-as-read actions. Like/comment activity rows have a 64px square post thumbnail on the left and text/timestamp on the right; the entire padded row is a native post link, with no separate View post label or nested profile link. Onboarding first collects a username and name, then requires a profile photo before showing main navigation. Unfinished onboarding resumes the photo step; completed member screens always show an actual profile photo. Choose profile photo / Change photo opens the file picker, followed directly by a native modal crop editor with a circular preview, one-finger drag positioning, two-finger pinch zoom and repositioning, arrow-key support, and a zoom slider. Use photo confirms the crop and saves it immediately; there is no separate upload form. Cancel or Escape discards an unsubmitted crop, and focus returns to the photo button.

Feed, detail, and upload-preview photos fill their container width at their full aspect ratio. Cap displayed height at twice the container width; unusually tall photos scale down within that area without cropping, rather than shrinking normal portraits to a viewport-height limit. Profile thumbnails use square crops. Photos in profile grids, feed photos, and Activity post links open a dedicated Post page, replacing the parent screen. It uses the feed's 520px desktop column, with a back link above the post and comments initially expanded below the photo. Feed headers show only the author's username and avatar alongside the timestamp. Heart and comment icons have accessible names and positive counts; the like count opens the people list. Comment composers share a pill-shaped neutral outline with a white send icon inside a Douglas Fir circle at the bottom right. The composer starts at 44px tall with a 22px left text inset; its 15px type matches post captions. The labeled textarea grows from one to six lines, then scrolls internally. The opaque 44px send circle is neutral gray while disabled and covers the outline at the bottom-right edges, and its plane icon is optically centered. Text-field focus darkens the existing single outline rather than adding an outer ring. There is no form margin above the composer. Space below it matches the gap from the feed divider to the next post header. Thin neutral separators replace raised card frames and tinted surfaces. Lists use explicit page controls. The final post page ends with a short, centered light-gray horizontal line and an accessible label, without a visible end-of-posts sentence. Preserve clear loading, empty, error, and confirmation states, visible keyboard focus, and controls at least 44px tall. No automatic animation, engagement gimmicks, or invented social activity.

Secondary pages have labeled arrow links above their content: Settings returns to your profile on mobile, while desktop uses the sidebar instead; editing, invitations, and blocked accounts return to Settings. Your own profile is a primary destination and has no back control. Other members’ profiles return to the prior in-app route, retaining a selected profile tab, with Feed as the fallback for a direct link. Photo details have matching back controls that retain the parent list page. These controls name their destination and work for direct links without relying on external browser history. Settings uses matching full-width rows, separators, and touch targets for links, disclosures, and actions. Navigation/disclosure rows have chevrons; deletion remains collapsed with explicit confirmation.

Routine refresh buttons are absent. Selecting an already active Feed, Activity, or own Profile destination starts a fresh first page and scrolls to the top. Selecting New post again preserves the draft, and modified clicks retain normal link behavior. Settings contains the single full-app Reload app control. Failed reads still offer Retry. The Activity icon carries a compact Douglas Fir green numbered bubble with white text (99+ above 99), also described to assistive technology. Its unified unread count refreshes on navigation, focus, reconnect, and once a minute while visible; this does not reload the feed or Activity page. All activity types remain interleaved. A notification summary must be at least half visible for 250 continuous milliseconds before acknowledgement; time in a hidden tab or behind mobile navigation does not qualify. There is no manual read control or separate request section.

The static document shows “Starting Slowmass…” with an independent reload guard if the application script fails to start. Once running, startup shows “Loading Slowmass…” until session verification completes, with a 15-second request deadline and a retry action on failure; the login form appears only after a confirmed signed-out response. Lists and detail screens distinguish loading, failure, and confirmed empty results. Refreshing or retrying clears stale read errors and results; current data appears after the read succeeds. Status text uses a polite live region, with no decorative loading animation.

Verification uses static checks and API/integration tests only. Browser testing, screenshot inspection, and visual regression testing are prohibited by the user.

Post likes and comments form a compact left-aligned row, with 24px icons, 14px semibold counts, a 7.2px icon-to-count gap and 19.2px between groups. Groups flow with their contents instead of occupying fixed columns. Keep icons left aligned inside their controls, including when counts are absent; keep the separate like-count target at least 24px wide and all controls at least 44px tall.

Post cards share a 16px vertical gap between the header avatar and photo, photo and caption line box, and caption and action icons. Account for the invisible space inside touch targets rather than adding it to the gap. Without a caption, use the same gap between photo and action icons.

Overflow menus dismiss when focus moves outside, the page scrolls, the viewport changes, or their actions become unavailable/change. Preserve keyboard navigation and return focus without scrolling the page back. Keep menus inside the viewport and above mobile navigation, opening upward when space below is insufficient; constrained menus scroll internally without dismissing.

## Branding comparison

`/branding/` preserves the earlier standalone, public, noindex palette comparison. It contains the original five PNW evergreen palette samples, rather than the current white application layout. Caveat and Douglas Fir were selected there; green is now limited to application primary actions and the approved app icon. These are artistic color directions, not botanical color specifications. Preview selections live only in the URL fragment and do not change the application theme or account data. Preview scripts load only on that page and make no API requests.

## Favicon

The selected favicon is the original lowercase `sm` monogram: Caveat bold in cream on a rounded Douglas Fir tile. `/favicon.svg` preserves the approved vector geometry. `/favicon.ico` contains 16px, 32px, and 48px versions; `/apple-touch-icon.png` is a 180px version with an opaque Douglas Fir background for home-screen use. These assets are linked from the application document. The temporary favicon comparison page was removed after selection.

## Installed app

The main document links `/manifest.webmanifest` for standalone launches from `/`. Browser theme and launch background colors are white. Home-screen installation uses the selected `sm` icon, including a maskable variant. On iPhone, iPad, and Android, Settings contains an expandable Install slowmass section with native installation when supported and manual mobile instructions otherwise. Desktop has no installation row, in-app install prompt, or device-setup row. An installed window shows its installed status instead. On phones and tablets, the device-setup suggestion is the first Activity row on the newest page: the app icon in the 64px square image column, a one-sentence summary, and inline right-side actions like follow requests—a Douglas Fir primary action (Show how, Turn on, or Install) and the same labeled × dismiss control as Follow back. It has no timestamp. iPhone/iPad Show how opens the shared modal dialog: a one-line reason, numbered steps with bold control names, a muted note for in-app browsers and existing installs, and a Done button. Settings uses the same numbered steps. Safe-area spacing accommodates the top status bar and bottom navigation. The app requires a network connection. It has no offline photo cache. Settings includes an explicit per-device Push notifications opt-in, with permission and unsupported-device guidance. Its push-only service worker does not intercept requests or cache private content. Magic links remain the primary login flow; only the installed app shows collapsed help for pasting an unused link when email opens a separate browser session.

Post headers center the avatar, username, compact relative time, and owner-only vertical three-dot menu on one row. Caption editing and deletion live in that menu and use native modal dialogs. Like counts open a modal with explicit pagination; its rows of people have whitespace only between rows, none before the first or after the last; they share the comment icon’s 7.2px icon-to-number gap. Comments use an avatar at the left, an inline bold username and body, a small relative timestamp and positive like total below, and a heart at the right. Keep comments flat, with no reply action. Use the shared 16px vertical gap from post action icons to the first comment, between comments, and before the composer, accounting for invisible touch-target padding. Keep list margins at zero. Metadata uses 20px text raised 4px above center in consistent 44px-high items, including timestamps without adjacent actions, bringing it closer to the comment body while preserving the gap below; adding a like total or delete control must not change the timestamp position or metadata row height. The profile's Invite friends shortcut uses an outline envelope to indicate an email invitation. Invitation history separates timestamps and actions with an explicit wrapping gap and shows email confirmation separately from invitation eligibility.

Onboarding uses a 420px column with one primary photo action, separated file/visibility help, and a quiet Use a different account link. Account deletion stays in Settings. Relationship controls use compact visible buttons inside 44px touch targets, with equal vertical spacing around profile follow controls. Following/follower rows include avatars and require confirmation before removal or unfollowing. Profile unfollow/block actions and comment deletion use the same confirmation pattern. The comment delete control is an accessible outline trash icon with a 44px touch target. Profiles and posts share the vertical overflow menu. Profiles place Unfollow for accepted follows above Block account in that menu; Request to follow and Cancel request remain inline. All modal surfaces share native focus handling and dismiss on backdrop clicks, except while saving; dragging from content never counts as a backdrop click. Post cropping uses an overlaid, freely resizable rectangle with keyboard support and no ratio presets or numeric fields.

Enlarged profile photos use the shared dialog’s image appearance: only the photo over a dark neutral backdrop, with no white panel or visible title. Keep the close control at the viewport’s top right, clear of device safe areas and the photo. The full image fits inside the viewport without cropping. Both Profile and Edit profile offer this viewer; other avatars retain profile navigation. Focused post photos reuse the viewer at the 900px desktop breakpoint, where posts narrow to 520px. The photo itself is the interactive surface, without a separate visible enlarge button; use a zoom-in cursor and native keyboard access. Full-width mobile photos remain plain images, and narrowing the screen closes any open post viewer.

Startup, startup retry/unavailability, and invitation-loading headers use a compact 26px wordmark on every viewport, retaining the 48px link target. The static pre-bundle shell uses the same rule. Normal app headers and OS-generated launch icons retain their existing size.

Invitations places Share an invite link before Invite by email. Show one primary Copy invite link action and a quiet expiry line. Copy reuses the current credential or creates one when absent/expired. Put Replace link inside the existing vertical three-dot overflow menu; confirmation explains that the previous link and outstanding verification emails stop working, and existing accounts remain. Show a selectable read-only URL after copying, including when clipboard access fails. Omit shared-link history, remaining-place counts, and per-link joiner panels. Joined-invitee Activity uses a full-row profile link with a circular private avatar, matching the existing 64px Activity image column. Shared joining explains email verification and optional following.

Every Activity image uses the same fixed 64px square footprint, including on narrow phones: profile avatars are circular and post thumbnails are square. Follow-request and new-follower Activity rows place the private avatar beside the linked summary and timestamp. Independent actions stay inline at the right and center vertically, with wrapping summary text on narrow phones. Accept and Follow back use Douglas Fir green with white text, while Deny and the labeled dismiss control stay neutral. Dismissing Follow back removes only that prompt, keeping the activity item.

Onboarding contains profile details and the required photo step only. Saving the photo opens the requested app page directly. Installation and notification controls live in Settings and the Activity device-setup row, and permission dialogs appear only after explicit opt-in.
